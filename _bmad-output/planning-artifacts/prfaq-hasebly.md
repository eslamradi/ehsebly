# No Sign-Up, No Calculator: Hasebly Splits the Bill From One Photo

## Egypt's 14% tax and 12% service, divided exactly by what each person ordered — not guessed, not fought over.

**Cairo, Egypt — [Launch Date]** — Hasebly launched today to end one specific, familiar moment: the bill lands at a group dinner, and someone has to work out — fast — who owes what. For the person who always ends up doing that math, hasebly turns a photo of the receipt into an exact, fair split in under a minute. No sign-up, no setup, no ledger to maintain.

The bill arrives, and for a second nobody moves. Someone has to add it up — the food, the drinks, then a 14% tax and a 12% service charge, none of which map cleanly onto what any one person actually ordered. Split it evenly, and whoever just had a Coke Zero quietly subsidizes whoever had the steak; nobody says anything, but everyone half-notices. Do it properly instead, and it means real math, out loud, at a table where everyone's already got a jacket on and a phone out, ready to pay and go. Either way, the dinner ends on the wrong note.

Now the fronter just points their phone at the receipt. Hasebly reads every item and price off it automatically, and a few taps later — this one's mine, that one's yours — everyone's exact share is on screen: their food, their drinks, and their fair slice of the tax and service, no more and no less. No calculator, no group-chat argument about who had what, no sign-up screen first. The fronter pays the bill, hasebly tells everyone else what they owe, and the table gets up and leaves.

> "I got tired of standing there with a calculator app open while my friends had their coats on, waiting for me to finish the math," said Radi, founder of hasebly. "Splitting a bill fairly shouldn't cost you the last five minutes of a good night out. I wanted something you point, tap, and forget — so the only thing anyone remembers about dinner is dinner."

### How It Works

1. **The bill lands.** The fronter opens hasebly and taps the camera icon — no sign-up screen first.
2. **Snap the receipt.** Hasebly reads the items and prices straight off the paper.
3. **Confirm what applies.** Hasebly shows what it read, and the fronter taps twice — is the 14% tax applicable here? Is the 12% service applicable? Some places charge both, some bake one or both into the price already, some charge neither. Two taps, and the split accounts for exactly what's real on this bill.
4. **Tap who had what.** The fronter goes down the list once — this drink's Sara's, this platter's split three ways.
5. **Review, then confirm.** Hasebly shows each person's exact total: their food and drinks, plus their proportional share of whatever tax and service actually apply. Anything OCR misread — tap it, fix it, same glance you'd give the real bill before paying anyway.
6. **Pay and go.** The fronter covers the bill, everyone else sends their share however they already do — InstaPay, Vodafone Cash, cash — and the table gets up.
7. **Look back later, if you need to.** Every split is saved under a private, anonymous ID — no sign-up required — so if a "did I already pay you back" question comes up next week, the answer's still there, even across a reinstall. Link an email or phone anytime, optional, to carry it across devices too.

> "Honestly I just wanted my Coke Zero to not count for anyone's steak. Took the photo, tapped four things, done. We were out the door before the waiter even brought the card machine back."
> — Mariam, 27, weekly dinner regular

### Getting Started

Hasebly is available today on iOS and Android, free to download and free to use — supported by ads, not subscriptions or fees on your split. Download it, open it, and point your camera at your next bill — no sign-up, nothing to set up first. The whole first-time experience, from download to seeing the split, takes less time than waiting for the check.

## Customer FAQ

### Q: If OCR gets an item or price wrong and I don't catch it before paying, who's responsible — and how would I even catch it in a flow designed to be fast?

A: Hasebly shows you the full itemized list — items, prices, and who's assigned to what — before the split is final. If OCR misread something (45 EGP read as 145), you tap it and fix it: the same glance you'd give the real bill before paying anyway. Speed comes from skipping the math, not from skipping your judgment. If nobody reviews before paying, that's the same risk as a manual split nobody double-checked either — hasebly isn't liable for what a receipt actually charged, it's a tool for computing the split accurately once the numbers are right.

### Q: It's ad-supported, and there's an anonymous account behind the scenes — so is hasebly really "no sign-up," or are you tracking me?

A: You never see a sign-up screen, never enter an email or password to use hasebly. Behind the scenes, we create a lightweight, anonymous ID for your install (a random username, not your name, phone, or email) so your split history survives a reinstall. You can optionally link an email or phone later if you want that history portable across devices — you're never asked to up front. Ads are matched to that anonymous ID for basic targeting, not to your name, number, or email unless you choose to link one.

### Q: My receipts are often faded thermal paper, mixed Arabic and English, sometimes handwritten additions — does the OCR actually work on that, or am I typing it in by hand anyway?

A: Honestly, this is the single biggest thing we haven't proven yet. OCR on clean, English, printed receipts is a solved problem elsewhere — Egyptian receipts are a harder case we're building and testing specifically for, not assuming will just work because it works in the US. What we can promise: the review step (see above) means a bad scan costs you a few taps to correct, not a full manual do-over. It's not all-or-nothing between "perfect OCR" and "back to a calculator."

### Q: Splitwise already scans receipts. Why would I switch to hasebly instead?

A: Splitwise is built to track shared expenses over time with the same group — you create an account, set up a group, add expenses, and settle up later. Hasebly is built for the one-off dinner with people you don't keep an ongoing ledger with: no group to set up, no waiting to settle later, the exact number before you leave the table, and everyone pays each other however they already do (InstaPay, Vodafone Cash, cash). If you run a persistent tab with the same roommates, Splitwise is still the right tool for that. Hasebly is for tonight.

### Q: Most of my friends just split the bill evenly without complaining. Why do I need an app for this?

A: You don't, if the unfairness doesn't bother anyone — most people eat the cost socially to avoid the awkwardness of itemizing. Hasebly is for the moment that stops being fine: someone's visibly paying more than they ordered, again, and somebody notices. It's not for every dinner — it's for the ones where the gap is big enough that someone would've done the math by hand anyway, except now it's two taps instead of five minutes of mental math at the table.

### Q: What about a shared appetizer or a bottle of wine three of us drank — your model sounds like one item, one person?

A: You can assign one item to multiple people — tap it once for each person who shared it, and hasebly splits that item's cost, plus its share of tax and service, evenly among just the people assigned to it. A mezze plate split three ways doesn't get lumped into everyone's total, only those three.

### Q: If I lose my phone or switch devices, do I lose my split history?

A: Your history is tied to your anonymous ID, not just the device, so reinstalling doesn't wipe it. Link an email or phone (optional, anytime) and it follows you across devices too. If you never link anything, it's still recoverable per-install, just not synced across multiple phones at once.

### Q: What happens to the actual photo of my receipt after you scan it?

A: The photo is used only to extract items and prices, then discarded — it isn't stored, and it isn't tied to your name unless you've linked an email or phone. What persists is the resulting split (items, amounts, who was assigned what), attached to your anonymous ID so your history survives a reinstall.

### Q: How much does this actually cost me, and are the ads going to make it not worth using?

A: Free to download, free to use — no subscription, no fee taken out of your split. Ads show after your split is complete, not interleaved into the scan-confirm-assign flow, so they don't cost you the speed that's the whole point of using hasebly at the table.

## Internal FAQ

### Q: The whole product rests on OCR reading messy, real Egyptian receipts. Has this actually been tested, or is this an unproven bet the entire concept is built on?

A: Unproven bet, honestly. De-risking plan: before building anything beyond the core loop, run a spike using an existing OCR service (Google Cloud Vision, Azure Document Intelligence, or a vision-capable LLM API) against real, deliberately varied receipts collected across the first friends-dinner tests — printed, thermal, faded, mixed Arabic/English, at least one with handwriting. If more than roughly 1 in 3 come back needing heavy correction even with the review-and-fix step as a safety net, that's the signal to narrow scope or rethink the OCR-first approach before investing further.

### Q: Would you actually pull this out mid-dinner, in front of your friends, and trust it enough to hold up the table while it works?

A: Yes — that's the actual validation plan, not a hypothetical. Threshold: 10 real dinners with friends. If by dinner 10 the founder is still reaching for hasebly instead of a calculator, and friends aren't waving it off for being too slow or too wrong, that's the signal it's real. If either of those things is happening by dinner 10, that's the signal to stop and rework before going further.

### Q: What's the smallest version worth building first, and what gets cut to hit that?

A: One platform only. Manual tap-assign, no smart suggestions. No local history, no anonymous ID, no account layer, no ads — none of it — for this first build. Just the core loop: scan receipt → confirm what tax/service applies → tap-assign items → review and fix → see the split. Everything described in the press release (anonymous ID, cross-device history, ads) is the destination once the 10-dinner test validates the core loop — not part of the first thing that gets built. This is a deliberately smaller scope than the full press-release vision, and that gap is intentional, not a shortfall.

### Q: What's the realistic path to the first 100 users, and why would they open hasebly instead of just splitting evenly like they already do?

A: Not marketing — the founder's own social graph. The first 10-20 users are friends, through real dinners, growing via word-of-mouth at the table ("wait, what app is this?") rather than paid acquisition, which isn't affordable yet on a free ad-supported app anyway. This growth loop shares its biggest risk with the OCR question above: it breaks immediately if the app embarrasses the founder at the table instead of impressing people, which is exactly why the OCR spike and the 10-dinner test come before anything else.

### Q: Ad revenue on a utility app someone might open once a week — what does that actually look like, and is it enough to justify the time being put into this?

A: At solo/single-market scale, not real income for a long while — realistically far below the cost of the founder's time. The bigger near-term concern: cloud OCR calls cost money per scan, and at low volume that cost could exceed ad revenue, meaning early economics are net-negative. Accepted trade-off: ads aren't the reason to build this right now, they're a way to not charge money while finding out if the core loop is wanted. Revenue isn't the v1 success metric — the 10-dinner trust threshold is.

### Q: If Splitwise (or a MENA fintech app) adds an Egypt tax preset in a sprint, what's actually left that's yours?

A: Thin, if the product is genuinely nothing more than OCR plus tax math — that combination is a copyable feature, not a moat. What's actually defensible: being first with real trust inside the Egyptian dining audience via word-of-mouth (slow to copy even when the feature isn't), and depth of local execution — properly handling Arabic/thermal receipts is a real investment a global player has less incentive to prioritize for one market. For an MVP, speed to real validated users is the honest strategy, not a durable technical secret.

### Q: Worst case — OCR is wrong often enough that people stop trusting the numbers and it gets around in the friend group. How would you find out, and what's the damage?

A: Because the first users are the founder's own friends, the feedback loop is immediate and direct — someone says "that's wrong" at the table, not a silent churn number weeks later. The real risk isn't failing to notice; it's continuing to use the flawed approach past the point friends have flagged repeated mistakes without a clear threshold to stop. The 10-dinner threshold above exists specifically to force that decision rather than let it drift.

### Q: Any legal or regulatory exposure — data privacy, or anything Egypt-specific about handling tax/service data?

A: Low at this stage — no in-app payments, no PII required, no account layer at all in the first build. The one real item to track: once linked-account functionality (email/phone) ships later, Egypt's data protection law (151/2020) applies to that identifiable data and needs a basic privacy notice — not a blocker now, just not something to skip when that feature actually ships.

## The Verdict

**Concept Strength:** Solid, MVP-ready concept with one clearly identified, unproven load-bearing risk — not yet "forged in steel," but forged pending one specific, already-scoped test.

**Forged in steel:**
- The customer and problem are sharp and specific — the bill-fronter, the Coke Zero fairness scene, Egypt's 14% tax + 12% service math. Passes the mom test cleanly; no jargon, no abstraction.
- Differentiation is real, not manufactured. When research revealed Splitwise Pro already has receipt OCR, the positioning correctly shifted to "no sign-up, one-off use, Egypt-specific handling" instead of quietly ignoring the conflict — a concept that bends under scrutiny instead of breaking under it is a strong signal.
- Two load-bearing correctness decisions emerged directly from hard questions, not from polish: the tax/service applicability toggle (prevents double- or under-charging when receipts vary in what they itemize) and the review-before-pay step (prevents a silent OCR misread from becoming a real money mistake between friends). The concept would have shipped without both if the process had moved faster.
- The validation plan is concrete and self-funded: a 10-dinner go/no-go threshold and a deliberately stripped-down first build (one platform, manual tap-assign, no accounts/history/ads), not a vague "we'll figure it out."

**Needs more heat:**
- Ad economics are directional, not proven. Cloud OCR cost-per-scan could exceed ad revenue at low volume; there's no answer yet for when that flips positive. Acceptable for a friends-only MVP; needs real numbers before scaling past that.
- The growth loop beyond the founder's immediate friend group is unproven — "word of mouth at the table" is plausible but untested with people who don't already trust the founder personally.

**Cracks in the foundation:**
- Competitive moat is thin, and that's acknowledged rather than resolved. If the 10-dinner test validates the concept, "what's actually defensible against a fast-follower" needs a real second answer before any launch wider than friends-of-friends.
- The "under a minute" end-to-end claim in the press release is still aspirational. It was deliberately kept in to force scrutiny during the Internal FAQ, and that scrutiny is still pending on the not-yet-executed OCR spike — this number may need to be walked back once real timing data exists.
- Legal/privacy work for the account-linking feature (Egypt's Law 151/2020) is correctly deferred past MVP, but deferred items are the ones that get skipped when things move fast — needs an explicit checklist entry before that feature ships, not a mental note.
